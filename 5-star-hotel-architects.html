Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can you design to a hotel operator's brand standards?
Yes. If you have signed, or are in talks with, a hotel operator, we design to their technical services guidelines from the concept stage: guest room dimensions, back-of-house areas, F&B and MEP provisions. Building these in from day one avoids expensive redesign when the operator reviews the drawings.
What does a 5-star hotel cost to build in India?
As a broad guide, an upscale 5-star property typically costs ₹1–1.8 crore per key to build, or roughly ₹6,000–9,000 per sq ft of built-up area. Luxury and boutique hotels with heavier finishes run higher. Location, basements, façade, finish level and the share of space given to F&B, banquets and spa all move the final number, so we work out a realistic figure for your site during the feasibility stage.
How long does a 5-star hotel project take?
Design and approvals typically take 6–12 months, and construction usually runs 24–36 months depending on key count, basements, site access and how much is fitted out before opening. Hotels can also open in phases, starting with the rooms and core public areas and adding banquets or spa later.
Do you handle just architecture, or interiors too?
Both. Our focus is the design side: architecture and interior design, from concept through construction-ready drawings. The lobby, guest rooms, suites, restaurants and spa are designed by the same studio that plans the building, so there is no coordination gap between separate architecture and interior firms.
Is there a minimum project size you take on?
Our hotel and hospitality projects typically start upward of ₹20 Crore. That lets us give every project full principal-architect attention and a proper planning process. We work across India and travel to new sites for the right project.
What makes Innov Architects different from other hotel architecture firms?
A FOAID 2025 award, 500+ projects delivered, and a hard rule: every project is personally overseen by principal architects, with no delegation of critical design decisions to junior staff. On a hotel, where hundreds of decisions affect both guest experience and operating cost, that continuity matters more than almost anything else.
