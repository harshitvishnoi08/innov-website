Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you only design hill resorts, or heritage and riverfront properties too?
Both. Our resort and hospitality portfolio spans hillside destinations like our Mukteshwar hill resort & villa township, and heritage riverfront properties like the Sangam Pooja Wellness Resort on the banks of the Ganga in Prayagraj. Whatever the terrain — hill, riverfront or heritage — we start by reading the land before designing anything.
Is there a minimum project size you take on?
Our resort and hospitality projects typically start upward of ₹5 Crore. This lets us commit full principal-architect attention and a proper master-planning process to every project we take on, rather than spreading thin across many small ones.
How long does a resort project take, from design to opening?
Design and approvals typically take 4–9 months, and construction of a full resort runs 18–30 months depending on size, terrain and access. Many resorts are built in phases — opening with a core of keys and the main public areas, then adding villas, spa and recreation as occupancy grows.
Do you handle just architecture, or interiors and construction too?
One studio for all three — architecture, interiors and construction supervision, concept through to handover. You don't need to coordinate between separate architecture and interior firms; our team stays with the project end to end.
Can you design a resort anywhere in India, or only in Uttarakhand and Himachal?
Pan-India. Our resort and hospitality projects span Uttarakhand, Himachal Pradesh, and Uttar Pradesh (including our heritage riverfront resort in Prayagraj), and we regularly travel to new sites anywhere in the country for the right project.
What makes Innov Architects different from other resort architecture firms?
A FOAID 2025 award, 500+ projects delivered, and a hard rule: every project is personally overseen by principal architects, with no delegation of critical design decisions to junior staff. For a resort project, that continuity from concept to construction matters more than almost anything else.
