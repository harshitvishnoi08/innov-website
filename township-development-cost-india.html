Township Development Cost
in India (2026)

Ar. Udit Vishnoi 4 September 2026 9 min read

Ask two developers what a township costs per acre and you'll get two very different answers — because "township cost" is actually three separate numbers people constantly mix up: land development, statutory development charges, and unit construction.

In our guide to planning a township in India, we walked through master planning, zoning and approvals. This guide breaks down the cost side specifically — what land development actually costs per acre, what EDC and IDC charges add, and what it costs to build the units themselves once the land is ready.

The Three Numbers That Make Up "Township Cost"

Before any figure means anything, it helps to separate what it's actually paying for:

  • Land development / infrastructure cost — grading the site and building roads, drainage, water and power networks, and common areas across the whole parcel.
  • Statutory development charges (EDC/IDC) — fees paid to the local development authority for external and internal infrastructure, calculated per acre.
  • Unit construction cost — the actual cost of building the homes, shops or other structures on the developed and serviced plots.

A quote that only covers one of these and gets compared against a quote covering all three will look wildly different — which is why most confusion about "township cost per acre" comes down to which of these three numbers is actually being discussed.

Land Development & Infrastructure Cost Per Acre

Industry estimates put basic township infrastructure development — roads, drainage, water and power distribution, and common area development — at roughly ₹75-80 lakh per hectare, which works out to approximately ₹30-32 lakh per acre. This is land development only; it does not include the cost of constructing any buildings.

These figures move significantly with terrain and soil condition — a hillside or soft-soil site needs far more retaining, grading and foundation work than a flat, stable plot — and with the infrastructure and open-space percentages mandated by the state's specific township policy, which can require anywhere from 10% to well over 30% of the land to be reserved for roads, parks and civic use before a single plot is sellable.

EDC & IDC — The Statutory Charges Behind the Headline Number

Two terms show up in almost every township cost conversation in India:

  • External Development Charges (EDC) — paid to the local development authority for infrastructure outside the project boundary: arterial roads, trunk water and power supply, and drainage that serves the wider area, not just this one project. EDC is typically calculated on a per-acre basis and, given the scale of a township, can add roughly 15-20% to overall project cost.
  • Internal Development Charges (IDC) — for infrastructure inside the project boundary: internal roads, on-site water supply and sewerage. Rates vary by state and by the local Department of Town and Country Planning (DTCP) or equivalent authority.

Both are set by the local authority, not by the developer or architect, and both must be verified for your specific state and district before they can be treated as more than a planning-stage estimate.

What It Costs to Build Within a Developed Township

Once land development is complete and plots are serviced, building the actual units follows the same construction economics as any other residential project. On a turnkey basis, that means roughly ₹1,800-2,200 per sq ft for economy quality, ₹2,200-3,000 for standard, ₹3,000-3,800 for premium, and ₹4,000-5,000+ per sq ft for luxury — the same bands covered in detail in our architect fees guide and construction cost content.

What changes inside a township is not the per-sq-ft construction rate — it's the shared infrastructure cost allocated to each unit, since the land development and EDC/IDC charges are spread across every plot in the development rather than borne by a single homeowner.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the cost of township development per acre in India?

Industry estimates put basic township infrastructure development at roughly ₹75-80 lakh per hectare, or approximately ₹30-32 lakh per acre. This covers land development only, not the construction cost of buildings, and varies with terrain, soil condition and the infrastructure mandated by the state's township policy.

What are EDC and IDC charges in a township project?

External Development Charges (EDC) are paid to the local development authority for infrastructure outside the project boundary, while Internal Development Charges (IDC) cover infrastructure inside the project boundary. EDC alone can add roughly 15-20% to overall project cost; both are calculated per acre based on the applicable authority rate.

How much does it cost to build within a township once the land is developed?

Individual unit construction follows standard residential rates — roughly ₹1,800-2,200 per sq ft for economy, ₹2,200-3,000 for standard, ₹3,000-3,800 for premium, and ₹4,000-5,000+ for luxury, on a turnkey basis. What changes inside a township is the shared infrastructure cost allocated to each unit, not the construction rate itself.

Why do township development costs vary so much between projects?

The biggest swing factors are terrain and soil condition, the state's mandated infrastructure and open-space percentages, how much land goes to roads and utilities versus sellable plots, and whether the site already has access to trunk infrastructure or needs it extended.

Get a Real Number for Your Site

Every figure in this guide is an industry-level planning estimate — useful for a first feasibility read, not a substitute for a site-specific study. Terrain, your state's township policy, and how much infrastructure your specific site already has will move the real number significantly in either direction.
