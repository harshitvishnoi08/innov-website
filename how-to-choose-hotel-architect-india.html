How to Choose a Hotel Architect in India
Checks, Fees, Questions & Red Flags

Ar. Udit Vishnoi 19 November 2026 12 min read

A hotel is a building that has to earn a living. Choosing its architect is less about finding the most impressive portfolio and more about finding the team that understands how a hotel works, what it costs and what it has to earn.

Search for hotel architects in India and you will find dozens of firms calling themselves the best. Very few of those pages tell you how to judge the claim. This guide is written from the other side of the table. We are hospitality architects ourselves, and these are the checks we would want any hotel owner to run on any firm, including ours.

It covers why hotel work is a specialist skill, the checks that matter, how fees and contracts usually work, the questions to ask in a first meeting, the warning signs, and how to decide between an Indian and an international firm. If you want to see how we approach hotels, our resort and hotel architecture practice and 5-star hotel architects pages show our work.

Why Hotel Architecture Is a Specialist Skill

A good residential or commercial architect is not automatically a good hotel architect. A hotel combines several building types in one: a home for guests, a restaurant, an event venue, a spa, an office and a factory for laundry and food. Each part has to work at full capacity on its busiest night, and all of them have to share the site without guests seeing the machinery.

Hotel design also connects directly to the business. The room count, the share of space given to banquets and restaurants, the efficiency of each floor and the phasing of construction all change what the hotel costs and what it earns. An architect who does not think in those terms can design a beautiful hotel that never pays back. Our guides to the hotel feasibility study and hotel master planning explain how deeply design and numbers are linked.

Seven Checks Before You Hire a Hotel Architect

1. Registration with the Council of Architecture

In India, only people with recognised qualifications who are registered with the Council of Architecture (COA) may use the title "Architect". In an August 2026 advisory reported by the Free Press Journal, the COA warned against unregistered people misusing the title under Sections 36 and 37 of the Architects Act, 1972, and advised developers and clients to verify registration on the Council's official website before engaging anyone. It takes a few minutes and is the first check to make.

2. Hospitality work of the kind you are building

Ask to see hotels and resorts the firm has designed, and look for ones close to yours. A city business hotel, a hill resort, a heritage conversion and a wedding-led property are very different projects. Ask which projects were built and which are concepts. There is nothing wrong with concept work, and much of any studio's portfolio is design that is still in development, but you should know which is which. On our own site, concept projects are labelled as concept renders.

3. Comfort with the numbers

A hotel architect should be able to talk about cost per key, area statements, room mix and phasing without hesitation, and give honest ranges early. Ask how they would estimate the cost of your hotel at concept stage, and how they keep the design within it.

4. Understanding of operations and back-of-house

Look at a plan from one of their hotels and ask how a room-service tray, a load of laundry and a wedding caterer's truck move through the building. A firm that has thought about this will answer at once. Ask how they size kitchens, stores and staff areas, and how they keep guest and service routes apart.

5. Knowledge of local rules and approvals

Hotels need change of land use, building plan approval, a fire NOC and often environmental clearance, and hill and coastal sites have extra rules. Ask which approvals the firm has handled and in which states. For a hill site, ask specifically about height limits, slope and seismic design, which we cover in our guide to mountain hotel architecture.

6. Experience with brand standards

If a hotel brand will operate the property, its technical standards govern room sizes, back-of-house, fire and services. Ask whether the firm has worked to brand standards before, and how early they want the operator involved. Bringing a brand in after the design is fixed usually means redesign.

7. References you can actually call

Ask for two or three past hotel clients and speak to them. Ask whether the design stayed within budget, how the firm handled changes, and whether the hotel works well now it is open. Contractors and project managers who have worked with the firm are also worth a call, because they see how complete and buildable the drawings were.

What to Look For, by Type of Hotel

The seven checks apply to every hotel, but each type of project puts weight on different skills. Use this to focus your questions.

Type of project Experience that matters most
City business hotel Compact, efficient floor plates, basement parking, meeting spaces, and working within tight urban building rules
Resort Master planning across a large site, villas or cottages, landscape, and climate-responsive design
Hill or mountain hotel Slope analysis, stepped buildings, seismic design, hill building rules and construction logistics
Heritage conversion Conservation, structural assessment of old buildings, and fitting modern services discreetly
Wedding or banquet-led hotel Banquet halls and lawns, kitchens for large events, parking, and separating event and guest traffic

What a Hotel Architect Should Deliver

When comparing proposals, make sure you are comparing the same scope. A full hotel engagement usually covers:

  • Site analysis and master plan. What the land can carry, where everything goes and how the hotel can grow.
  • Concept design. Room mix, area statement, massing and the design idea.
  • Design development. Detailed plans, elevations, materials and coordination with structural and services engineers.
  • Approval drawings. Drawings and documents for the local authority and the fire department.
  • Working drawings. The technical drawings and details a contractor builds from.
  • Interiors and landscape. Either in-house or through specialist consultants the architect coordinates.
  • Site supervision. Regular visits during construction to check the work matches the design.

Some firms quote for design and approval drawings only. Others include interiors, landscape and supervision. Either can be right, as long as you compare like with like. A low quote that covers less often costs more by the end.

Fees and Contracts

Architect fees in India are usually a percentage of construction cost. Our guide to architect fees in India covers this in detail. In brief:

  • Typical range. In practice, fees for commercial projects commonly run about 6–12% of construction cost, above the COA minimum rates. Hotels sit at the more complex end of commercial work.
  • Staged payments. A common structure is about 10–15% at concept design, 30–40% at design development, 30–40% at working drawings, and the balance on completion.
  • Supervision. Where construction supervision is included, it is often billed separately, commonly around an additional 50% of the design fee, paid monthly during construction.
  • Taxes and extras. GST at 18% is payable on the professional fee.

Whatever the fee, put the scope, stages, payments, number of design revisions, and what happens if the project is paused or changed in writing. Payments should follow completed stages of work, not the calendar.

How to Compare Proposals

Once you have two or three proposals, it is tempting to compare the fee and nothing else. A simple scoring sheet gives a fairer picture. Score each firm out of five on each line, and weight the lines that matter most for your project.

What to score What a strong answer looks like
Relevant work Built or well-developed hospitality projects similar to yours, with plans as well as images
Understanding of your brief Questions about your guests, revenue mix and phasing, not only about style
Cost thinking Honest cost-per-key ranges and a clear way of keeping the design within budget
Scope Every stage listed, with interiors, landscape and supervision clearly in or out
Team A named lead architect and a team you will actually work with
References Past clients who are happy to speak, and say the budget and timeline held
Fee A fee in line with the scope, paid against completed stages

The cheapest proposal often scores lowest on scope, which means extra fees later. The most expensive is not always the best either. The firm that scores well across all seven lines is usually the safest choice.

Questions to Ask in the First Meeting

  1. Which hotels or resorts similar to mine have you designed, and which of them are built?
  2. Who in your team would lead my project, and who would I deal with week to week?
  3. How would you estimate the cost per key at concept stage, and how do you keep the design within budget?
  4. How do you plan back-of-house, service routes and staff areas?
  5. Which approvals have you handled for hotels, and in which states?
  6. Have you worked with a hotel brand's technical standards before?
  7. Do you design interiors and landscape in-house, or with partners you coordinate?
  8. What exactly is included in your fee, and what is extra?
  9. How many design revisions are included, and how are changes charged?
  10. Can I speak to two of your past hotel clients?

Red Flags to Watch For

  • No COA registration, or reluctance to share the registration number.
  • Only renders, never plans. Beautiful images with no floor plans or explanation of how the hotel works.
  • No talk of cost. A firm that avoids cost ranges until the design is finished.
  • Every project the same. A single style applied everywhere, whatever the site, climate or market.
  • Unclear scope. A low quote that leaves out working drawings, coordination or supervision without saying so.
  • No references. Past clients who cannot be contacted.
  • Promises about approvals. Guarantees of approval timelines that no architect can control.

Indian Firm or International Hospitality Firm?

For large branded hotels, owners sometimes consider an international hospitality architecture firm. Both routes can work, and many large projects combine them.

International firm Indian hospitality firm
Experience of global brands and large flagship hotels Knowledge of local rules, approvals, costs and construction practice
Strong for concept design and brand-level positioning Present on site and available through construction
Higher fees and travel costs, and time-zone gaps Fees in line with Indian market rates
Usually works with a local architect for approvals and working drawings Can carry the project from concept to completion

A common model on big projects is an international design architect for the concept with an Indian firm as the architect of record, preparing approval and working drawings and supervising on site. For most hotels and resorts in India, including mid-scale, boutique, wedding-led and hill properties, an experienced Indian hospitality firm can handle the whole project.

Architect, Consultant or Interior Designer?

Hotel projects usually involve three kinds of specialist, and it helps to know who does what:

  • Hospitality consultant. Market study, financial feasibility, positioning and operator selection.
  • Architect. Site, master plan, building design, approvals, working drawings and coordination of engineers.
  • Interior designer. Rooms, public spaces, materials, furniture, lighting and finishes.

Some firms combine architecture and interiors, which keeps one team responsible for the whole guest experience. Others separate them. If you hire them separately, decide early who leads and how they coordinate.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose a hotel architect in India?

Check that the architect is registered with the Council of Architecture, then look for hospitality projects similar to yours and ask which are built. Test their understanding of cost per key, back-of-house planning, brand standards and local approvals, compare proposals on the same scope, and speak to past hotel clients before deciding.

How do I check if an architect is registered in India?

Use the Council of Architecture's official website, which lists registered architects. The COA has advised clients and developers to verify registration there before engaging anyone, because only registered persons with recognised qualifications may use the title "Architect" under the Architects Act, 1972.

How much does a hotel architect charge in India?

Fees are usually a percentage of construction cost. For commercial projects, including hotels, fees commonly run about 6–12% of construction cost, above the COA minimum rates, paid in stages from concept to completion. Construction supervision is often billed separately, and 18% GST applies to the professional fee.

Should I hire an international firm for my hotel?

For a large branded flagship, an international design architect working with an Indian architect of record can make sense. For most hotels and resorts in India, an experienced Indian hospitality firm can handle the whole project, with better knowledge of local rules and costs and a stronger presence on site.

Do I need a hospitality consultant as well as an architect?

On larger hotels, yes. The consultant studies the market and finances and recommends the hotel's positioning, while the architect tests that brief against the site and the rules and designs the building. For a smaller hotel or resort, many owners start with an experienced hospitality architect and bring in a consultant for the financial study.

Choose the Team, Not Just the Portfolio

The right hotel architect understands guests, staff, costs, rules and the business as well as design. Check registration, look closely at relevant work, ask hard questions about numbers and operations, read the scope carefully and speak to past clients. Those checks take a few hours. Living with the wrong building lasts decades.
