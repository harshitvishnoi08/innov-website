Serviced Apartment Architecture in India
Units, Rules and Building Planning

Ar. Udit Vishnoi 24 November 2026 12 min read

A guest staying three nights wants a good hotel room. A guest staying three weeks wants a kitchen, a washing machine, somewhere to work and a place that feels like home. Serviced apartments are designed for the second guest.

Serviced apartments sit between a hotel and a home. They offer furnished, self-contained units with hotel services such as housekeeping, security and a reception, for stays that run from a week to several months. In India they serve professionals on project assignments, families relocating to a new city, patients and their families travelling for treatment, and growing numbers of remote workers.

This guide explains serviced apartment architecture for Indian projects: what serviced apartments are, the building models, the rules, unit sizes and layouts, building planning, operations and location. It sits alongside our guides to branded residences and hotel room sizes in India.

What Is a Serviced Apartment?

A serviced apartment is a furnished unit with its own kitchen or kitchenette, a living area and one or more bedrooms, rented for longer stays with hotel-style services. The key differences from other formats:

FormatTypical stayWhat guests get
Hotel roomOne to a few nightsA bedroom and bathroom, daily housekeeping, restaurants
Serviced apartmentA week to several monthsKitchen, living space, laundry, regular housekeeping, reception
Branded residenceOwner-occupied or rentedA home owned by the buyer, managed to a brand's standards
Co-livingMonths to a yearA private room with shared kitchens and lounges, community programmes

Singapore gives a useful benchmark for how regulators define the format. Its planning authority, the URA, requires serviced apartment units to be self-contained with kitchens or kitchenettes, at least 35 sq m net, rented for a minimum of seven days and held under one ownership, with no strata subdivision. Indian rules differ, but the benchmark is useful because it treats a serviced apartment as one managed lodging product.

Who Uses Serviced Apartments in India

  • Project and corporate stays. Engineers, consultants and managers posted to a city for weeks or months.
  • Relocating families. People moving to a new city who need a base while they find a home and schools.
  • Medical travellers. Patients and families staying near major hospitals during treatment and recovery.
  • Leisure and remote workers. Longer holidays and work-from-anywhere stays in hill and coastal destinations.

Hotel groups are responding. In September 2026, Deccan Chronicle reported that Sheraton Hyderabad added 42 apartments, taking its inventory to 98 apartments alongside 228 hotel rooms. The new units include studios of 35 sq m, one-bedroom apartments of 61 sq m and two-bedroom apartments of 88 sq m, each with a washer, a dishwasher and a drinking-grade water supply. The hotel linked the expansion to guests whose travel needs go beyond the traditional short stay, driven by office growth in the city.

Building Models

  • Standalone serviced apartment building. A whole building of apartments with a reception, amenities and back-of-house.
  • A block within a hotel. Apartments in a separate wing or tower, sharing the hotel's restaurants, pool and services.
  • Apartments within an office or commercial complex. Units for visiting staff and executives, close to where they work.
  • Apartments in a mixed-use scheme. Part of a larger development with offices, retail and homes. Our guide to mixed-use development covers how uses share a site.

The Rules: What the UP Regulations Allow

Rules vary by city and state, so check the master plan and bye-laws that apply to your site. The Uttar Pradesh Building Regulations, 2026, which cover NOIDA, Greater Noida, YEIDA and the state's other industrial development authorities, treat service apartments as a distinct use:

ProvisionWhat the regulations say
Within hotelsService apartments may be built as a separate block in hotel buildings
Within other buildingsAllowed in commercial and office complexes and institutional buildings up to 20 per cent of FAR
Standalone buildingsUp to 20 per cent of the FAR may be used for offices, conference facilities, retail and guest rooms
Plot size and accessNo minimum plot size, minimum existing road width of 12 m
FARBase FAR of 2.5, with the maximum rising on wider roads through purchasable FAR
Parking1.25 equivalent car spaces per 100 sq m of proposed FAR, the same as hotels

The 20 per cent allowance in standalone buildings is useful. It lets a serviced apartment project include a business centre, meeting rooms, a café or shop and some short-stay rooms, which makes the building work better for long-stay guests.

Unit Mix and Sizes

Most serviced apartment buildings offer three or four unit types:

Unit typeWho it suitsReference size
StudioSolo professionals, short project stays35 sq m (Sheraton Hyderabad, and Singapore's minimum)
One bedroomCouples, longer assignments61 sq m (Sheraton Hyderabad)
Two bedroomFamilies, colleagues sharing88 sq m (Sheraton Hyderabad)
Three bedroomLarger families, relocation staysSized to the market

For comparison, a standard Indian hotel room is about 200 to 250 sq ft, according to our hotel room size guide. A 35 sq m studio is about 377 sq ft, so even the smallest serviced apartment is noticeably larger than a hotel room, because it has to hold a kitchen and a place to sit and eat.

The right mix depends on the market. A building near an IT corridor will lean towards studios and one-bedrooms. A building near a large hospital needs more two-bedroom units for families. A hill or beach location may need larger units for family holidays.

Designing the Apartment

A long-stay guest lives in the unit, so it has to work like a small home and be durable like a hotel room.

Kitchen

More than anything else, the kitchen sets a serviced apartment apart from a hotel room. Studios usually get a compact kitchenette along one wall, larger units a proper kitchen. Plan for a hob, a microwave, a refrigerator, a sink and enough storage for a few weeks of groceries. Indian cooking needs good exhaust, so plan a ducted hood or a window close to the hob. Decide early between gas and induction. Induction avoids gas piping in every unit and simplifies fire safety.

Laundry

Long-stay guests wash clothes. A washer, or a washer-dryer, in each unit or a shared laundry room on each floor is essential. Indian guests also want space to dry clothes, so a small utility balcony helps where the facade allows it.

Living and working

  • Separate sleep from living. Even in a studio, a partition, a step in the ceiling or a sliding screen makes the space feel like more than a bedroom.
  • A proper work desk. Many guests work from the apartment, so give them a desk with good light and power points.
  • A dining table. Long-stay guests eat at home. A table for two to four is better than a breakfast bar.
  • Storage. Full-height wardrobes, luggage storage and a shoe cabinet near the door.

Finishes and services

  • Durable surfaces that housekeeping can clean quickly, with hotel-grade fittings.
  • Drinking water treated in the unit or supplied from a central plant.
  • Sound insulation between units and from corridors, since guests stay long enough to notice noise.
  • Individual metering where the operator wants to charge for electricity on long stays.

Interiors That Feel Like Home

Hotel rooms can afford to feel like hotel rooms. An apartment where someone lives for a month cannot. A few choices make a big difference:

  • Warm, layered lighting. Ceiling light for cleaning and cooking, plus floor and table lamps for the evening, instead of one bright downlight grid.
  • Soft furnishings. Rugs, curtains and cushions that make the unit feel lived-in, chosen in fabrics that housekeeping can wash or replace.
  • Local character. Art, textiles and materials from the region give each building an identity, instead of the anonymous look common in older serviced apartments.
  • Room to personalise. Open shelves and a pinboard let guests add photos and books, which matters on long stays.
  • Comfortable seating. A proper sofa and an armchair, since guests spend their evenings in the unit too.

Planning the Building

Arrival and reception

Guests check in once and stay for weeks, so the reception can be smaller than a hotel's. It still needs to feel secure and staffed, with a lounge, a concierge desk and space for luggage.

Deliveries

Long-stay guests in Indian cities order groceries, food and parcels every day. A parcel room or locker wall near the entrance, with a route for delivery staff that does not pass through the residents' lobby, keeps the building secure and calm.

Amenities

  • A gym and, where the budget allows, a pool.
  • A lounge or co-working space for guests who work remotely.
  • A children's room and outdoor play space for families.
  • A small café or breakfast room, since many guests skip cooking in the morning.

Back-of-house

Housekeeping visits are less frequent than in a hotel, but linen, laundry, stores and maintenance still need space. A service lift and linen rooms on each floor keep trolleys out of guest corridors. Our back-of-house planning guide covers the details.

Parking

Long-stay guests often bring or rent cars. Under the UP regulations, parking is 1.25 equivalent car spaces per 100 sq m of proposed FAR. Add space for drivers to wait and for taxis to drop off.

Approvals, Fire Safety and Accessibility

A serviced apartment building is usually treated as a lodging use, close to a hotel, not as ordinary housing. That affects several approvals:

  • Land use and building permit. Confirm that serviced apartments are allowed on the plot under the master plan, and use the correct occupancy in the building plans.
  • Fire safety. Lodging buildings need fire detection, alarms, sprinklers where required, protected staircases and exits sized for the occupancy. Kitchens in every unit add to the fire load, which is one more reason to consider induction cooking.
  • Accessibility. Ramps at entrances, accessible lifts and at least some units designed for guests with disabilities.
  • Operating licences. Depending on the state, lodging, police and health registrations, plus food licences if there is a café or breakfast room.

Designing for Operations

  • Housekeeping. Plan for weekly or twice-weekly cleaning, with linen changes and a full deep clean between stays.
  • Turnover. When a long stay ends, the unit needs a fast reset. Simple furniture and finishes that are easy to repair help.
  • Maintenance. Kitchens and washing machines need more maintenance than a hotel room. Plan access panels and service routes for plumbing.
  • Single management. Buildings run by one operator perform best. Where units are sold to investors, put all of them into a single rental programme with clear rules.
  • Refurbishment. Choose finishes and fittings that can be replaced unit by unit without closing the building.

Choosing the Location

Serviced apartments live on long-stay demand, which makes location even more critical than for a hotel.

  • Office and IT clusters where companies send staff on assignments.
  • Large hospitals that draw patients from other cities and countries.
  • Industrial corridors and new townships where projects run for months and hotel supply is thin.
  • Airports and transport hubs for crew and transit stays.
  • Hill and coastal destinations for longer leisure and remote-work stays.

A feasibility study should test demand, competing supply and likely stay lengths before the unit mix is fixed. Our guide to the hotel feasibility study explains how this works.

Converting an Existing Building

Some of the best serviced apartment projects are conversions. Older hotels, office buildings and apartment blocks can all become serviced apartments if the structure and services allow it.

  • Hotels. Combining two rooms into one apartment adds a kitchen and living space without changing the structure. The main work is plumbing and exhaust for the new kitchens.
  • Office buildings. Deep office floor plates can be hard to light and ventilate as apartments. Narrow buildings or those with courtyards convert more easily.
  • Apartment blocks. The units already have kitchens, so the work is mostly upgrading finishes, adding a reception and back-of-house and meeting lodging fire rules.

Before committing, check the permitted use, the structure, the plumbing risers and the fire escape routes. A conversion that needs new risers on every floor can cost almost as much as a new building.

Common Serviced Apartment Mistakes

  • Hotel rooms with a kettle. Units without a real kitchen, laundry or storage fail long-stay guests.
  • No exhaust for Indian cooking. Smells spread into corridors and soft furnishings.
  • Nowhere to work. A bed and a TV but no proper desk.
  • Deliveries through the lobby. Daily food and parcel traffic mixed with guests.
  • Strata-sold units with no common operator. Inconsistent quality and service across the building.
  • Wrong unit mix. Studios near a hospital that needs family units, or large units in an IT corridor.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a serviced apartment and a hotel?

A hotel room is designed for short stays and has a bedroom and bathroom, with daily housekeeping and restaurants. A serviced apartment adds a kitchen or kitchenette, living space and laundry, and is designed for stays of a week to several months, with regular but less frequent housekeeping.

How big should a serviced apartment be?

Studios are commonly around 35 square metres, which is also Singapore's legal minimum for serviced apartments. Sheraton Hyderabad's recently added units are 35 square metres for studios, 61 for one-bedroom and 88 for two-bedroom apartments. The right size depends on the market and stay length.

Are serviced apartments allowed in commercial buildings in Noida?

Under the UP Building Regulations, 2026 for industrial development authority areas such as NOIDA and Greater Noida, service apartments are allowed in commercial and office complexes and institutional buildings up to 20 percent of FAR, and as a separate block within hotel buildings.

How much parking does a serviced apartment building need?

The UP Building Regulations, 2026 set parking for hotels and service apartments at 1.25 equivalent car spaces per 100 square metres of proposed FAR. Other cities have their own rules, so check the local bye-laws.

Should serviced apartments have a full kitchen?

Studios usually have a compact kitchenette with a hob, microwave, refrigerator and sink, while larger units have a full kitchen. For Indian cooking, a ducted exhaust hood or a window near the hob is important, and induction cooking can simplify fire safety by avoiding gas piping in every unit.

Design for the Guest Who Stays

A serviced apartment succeeds when a guest can live in it comfortably for weeks: cook, wash, work, rest and receive deliveries without friction. That needs home-like layouts built to hotel standards, a building planned around long-stay routines and an operator who runs it as one product.
