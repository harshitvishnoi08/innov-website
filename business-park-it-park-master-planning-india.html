Business Park and IT Park Master Planning
Planning for Tenants You Have Not Met Yet

Ar. Udit Vishnoi 24 November 2026 12 min read

A business park or IT park is a long bet. The land is bought today, the first tenants arrive in three years and the last buildings may go up a decade later. The master plan has to work for tenants nobody has met yet.

Business parks and IT parks bring offices for many companies onto one planned site, with shared infrastructure, amenities and a common identity. In India they range from IT parks of a few buildings in tier-2 cities to large integrated technology townships with homes, schools and retail alongside the offices. Demand from global capability centres (GCCs), technology firms and Indian corporates has made them one of the most active parts of commercial real estate.

This guide explains business park and IT park master planning for Indian projects. It covers definitions, demand, policy and rules, site selection, master plan structure, buildings, amenities, infrastructure, mobility, phasing and leasing. For single-company sites, see our companion guide to corporate campus architecture.

Business Parks, IT Parks and IT Cities

The terms overlap, and the differences affect both planning and incentives. The IT and ITeS Policy of Uttar Pradesh, 2022 gives useful working definitions:

TypeWhat it is
Business parkA multi-tenant office park for a range of businesses, with shared infrastructure and amenities
IT parkUnder the UP policy, a development with a minimum floor area of about 15,000 sq m, with at least 75 per cent of the allocable area for IT activities and IT infrastructure such as fibre and broadband connectivity
IT cityUnder the UP policy, 100 to 500 acres used roughly 60:40 for processing (IT units) and non-processing areas such as housing, commercial, education, healthcare and open space
Integrated IT TownshipUnder the UP Building Regulations, 2026, a land parcel of at least 10 hectares (25 acres) with an existing access road of at least 18 m
Corporate campusA site planned for one organisation

The UP policy also describes an IT city as one that promotes a walk-to-work culture, combining IT offices and co-working with residential and commercial complexes, food courts, gyms, conference halls and transport facilities.

Why Demand Is Strong

Global capability centres have become the largest single driver of office demand in India. According to Colliers India, as reported by IBEF in September 2025, GCCs were projected to make up 40 per cent of India's office space market in 2025 and have absorbed nearly 100 million sq ft across the top seven cities since 2021. CBRE found that global corporates increasingly prefer newer, green-certified integrated tech parks for their Indian set-ups. That preference rewards well-planned parks with strong infrastructure and amenities over standalone buildings.

Policy and Rules in Uttar Pradesh

Policies and rules differ by state. Uttar Pradesh, which includes Noida and Greater Noida, has some of the most detailed provisions, and they show the kind of framework developers should expect.

UP IT and ITeS Policy, 2022

  • Capital support. One-time support of 25 per cent of eligible capital expenditure for a greenfield IT park, up to ₹20 crore, for one IT park in each revenue division except the districts of Gautam Buddh Nagar and Ghaziabad. Capital expenditure covers building and infrastructure, not land.
  • Payment linked to leasing. 80 per cent of the support is released in instalments with construction progress, and the last 20 per cent after half the built-up floor area is leased.
  • Stamp duty. 100 per cent exemption on stamp duty for land bought or leased by the developer.
  • FAR. FAR of 3 plus 1 purchasable for IT parks, subject to the building bye-laws in force.
  • Co-working. IT parks that host co-working spaces are also eligible.

Private Business Park Development Scheme

Invest UP's summary of the state's business park scheme sets out rules for parks developed on state land through private partners:

  • A minimum land parcel of 10 acres, with a 45-year concession and an option to extend for another 45 years.
  • Partial FAR completion within two years and full FAR within three years of the start date.
  • Parks up to 10 acres built in a single phase, 10 to 15 acres in up to two phases and larger parks in three or more phases, subject to approval.
  • At least 50 per cent reserved for IT, ITeS and GCCs, and no more than 10 per cent for non-core amenities such as food courts, retail kiosks and recreation.

Building regulations

The UP Building Regulations, 2026 treat IT offices as office buildings: a minimum 12 m access road, base FAR of 2.5 rising with road width, 6 to 16 m between blocks depending on height, parking of 2.0 equivalent car spaces per 100 sq m of FAR in metros and a free bonus of 5 to 7 per cent extra FAR for green-rated buildings. Our corporate campus guide sets these out in a table.

SEZ rules

Many IT parks are developed as Special Economic Zones. Since December 2023, a new Rule 11B in the SEZ Rules lets IT/ITeS SEZ developers, with Board of Approval consent, demarcate part of their built-up area as non-processing area for non-SEZ IT businesses, according to an EY tax alert. Developers must keep a minimum processing area and repay the proportionate tax benefits on the demarcated space. Plan buildings so floors can be separated cleanly if this option may be used.

Choosing the Site

  • Connectivity. Close to an expressway, metro line or airport, with routes from where the workforce lives.
  • Road width. Access roads wide enough to unlock the FAR the business plan needs. In UP, integrated IT townships need an 18 m existing road.
  • Utilities. Capacity for heavy power loads, reliable water and multiple fibre routes.
  • Size and shape. Enough area for the full programme and a regular shape that divides into efficient plots.
  • Surroundings. Housing, schools, hotels and retail nearby, or space to build them, so employees can live close by.

Structuring the Master Plan

A business park master plan has to stay flexible for decades while still feeling like one place from the first phase. The structure we use:

  • A clear spine. A main boulevard or green spine that organises the plots, carries utilities and gives the park its identity.
  • A central amenity hub. Food courts, a gym, a crèche, a convention or training centre and retail grouped at the heart of the park, within walking distance of every building.
  • Plots of different sizes. Large plots for built-to-suit headquarters and smaller ones for multi-tenant buildings, so the park can serve different occupiers.
  • Gateways and edges. A strong entrance, a landscaped edge and consistent signage that make the park recognisable.
  • Service routes. Loading, waste and utility access along the back of plots, away from the main pedestrian routes.
  • Reserved land. Space for future buildings, a hotel or serviced apartments and expansion of the amenity hub.

Designing the Buildings

Multi-tenant buildings

  • Divisible floor plates. Floors that can be let whole or split into two to four tenancies, with cores placed to allow that.
  • Efficient depth. Plates deep enough for flexible layouts but shallow enough for daylight.
  • Generous floor-to-floor heights for raised floors, ducts and good ceilings.
  • Warm shell delivery. Many parks hand over floors with finished cores, toilets and services, ready for tenant fit-out.

Built-to-suit buildings

Larger tenants may want their own building designed to their standards. Plan plots and infrastructure so these can be added without disrupting the rest of the park.

Character

Consistent design guidelines for height, materials, facade shading, signage and landscape keep the park coherent as different buildings are added over the years.

Amenities

  • Food courts and cafés. Large-scale dining with several counters. Our F&B planning guide covers kitchens and food courts.
  • Convention and training. A shared conference and training centre that tenants can book.
  • Wellness. A gym, sports courts, walking tracks and a medical centre.
  • Family support. A crèche near the park entrance.
  • Hospitality. A business hotel or serviced apartments for visiting staff.
  • Retail and services. Banks, pharmacies and convenience stores, within whatever share the scheme allows.

Infrastructure

Infrastructure is what tenants check first and what is hardest to change later.

  • Power. On-site substations, dual feeds where available and backup generation for critical loads.
  • Data. Multiple fibre routes into the park from different directions, and ducts to every plot.
  • Cooling. A district cooling plant can serve several buildings more efficiently than separate systems.
  • Water and waste. Treatment plants, recycled water for flushing and landscape, rainwater harvesting and organised solid waste handling.
  • Utility corridors. Services laid in planned corridors or trenches along roads, so plots can connect without digging up the park.

Mobility

  • Public transport. Shaded walking routes or shuttles from the nearest metro or bus stop.
  • Company buses and cabs. Bays at each building or at a central transport hub, with covered waiting.
  • Two-wheelers. Large shaded parking near entrances.
  • Car parking. To local rules, in basements, multi-level car parks or shared parking structures.
  • EV charging in parking areas from the start.
  • Walkability. Shaded, continuous footpaths between buildings and amenities, so people can walk to lunch instead of driving.

Security and Estate Management

A business park is run like a small town, and the master plan should make that easy.

  • Layered security. A park-level perimeter and gates, with each building controlling its own entry. Tenants with strict security needs can add a further layer inside their floors.
  • A central command room. One place for CCTV, fire alarms, building management and incident response across the park.
  • Visitor and vendor routes. A visitor centre at the gate, and delivery routes that keep trucks away from pedestrian areas.
  • Estate services. Space for the facility management team, stores, a maintenance workshop and a waste yard.
  • Shared costs. Clear rules for how common infrastructure and amenities are maintained and charged to tenants.

Sustainability

Green ratings are now expected by many global tenants, and in Uttar Pradesh they also bring extra FAR. A park can go further than a single building, with shared solar, district cooling, recycled water and landscape that cools the site. Our guide to climate-responsive design covers shading and passive strategies that apply to office buildings too.

Walk-to-Work Housing

The UP IT and ITeS Policy describes IT cities as places that promote a walk-to-work culture, with residential and commercial complexes alongside the offices. Large parks increasingly include, or sit next to, housing for the people who work there. The UP Building Regulations, 2026 also allow the Authority to permit up to 40 per cent of the availed FAR on commercial plots of 2 to 4 hectares for residential use, subject to an infrastructure plan.

Housing close to the offices cuts commutes, keeps the park busy after office hours and helps tenants recruit. It needs its own entrances, green space, schools and shops nearby, and a clear boundary from the office areas. Our guides to mixed-use development and township planning cover how homes and workplaces share a site.

Phasing and Leasing

Business parks are built in phases, and the first phase sells the whole park. It should include the entrance, at least one finished office building, part of the amenity hub and finished landscape along the main spine. Tenants will judge the park by what they see on their first visit.

  • Anchor tenant. An early large tenant can justify infrastructure and attract others.
  • Infrastructure ahead of buildings. Roads, utilities and the spine built for the full park, even if buildings follow later.
  • Flexible later phases. Plots that can switch between multi-tenant, built-to-suit, hotel or residential use as demand changes.
  • Timelines. Where schemes set FAR completion deadlines, as UP's business park scheme does, phase the design to meet them.

Common Business Park Mistakes

  • Infrastructure sized for phase one. Power and data that cannot support the full park.
  • Amenities left for later. Early tenants arrive to a park with nowhere to eat.
  • One plot size. No room for the large tenant who wants a building of its own.
  • Car-only design. No shaded walking routes, bus bays or two-wheeler parking.
  • Inconsistent buildings. No design guidelines, so the park looks unplanned within a few years.
  • Ignoring incentive conditions. Missing leasing, FAR or phasing milestones that incentives depend on.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a business park and an IT park?

A business park is a multi-tenant office park for a range of businesses. An IT park is focused on IT and ITeS activities with dedicated technology infrastructure. Under the UP IT and ITeS Policy, 2022, an IT park has a minimum floor area of about 15,000 square metres, with at least 75 percent of the allocable area for IT activities.

How much land does an IT city need in Uttar Pradesh?

The UP IT and ITeS Policy, 2022 describes an IT city as needing 100 to 500 acres, used roughly 60:40 for IT units and non-processing areas such as housing, commercial, education, healthcare and open space. The UP Building Regulations, 2026 separately require at least 10 hectares, or 25 acres, for an Integrated IT Township.

What incentives does Uttar Pradesh offer for IT parks?

The UP IT and ITeS Policy, 2022 offers one-time support of 25 percent of eligible capital expenditure for a greenfield IT park up to 20 crore rupees, in each revenue division except Gautam Buddh Nagar and Ghaziabad, plus full stamp duty exemption on land and FAR of 3 plus 1 purchasable.

What is the minimum size for a business park under UP's scheme?

Invest UP's summary of the state's private business park scheme sets a minimum land parcel of 10 acres, a 45-year concession, partial FAR within two years and full FAR within three years, with at least 50 percent reserved for IT, ITeS and GCC use.

Can SEZ IT parks lease space to non-SEZ companies?

Since December 2023, Rule 11B of the SEZ Rules allows IT and ITeS SEZ developers, with Board of Approval consent, to demarcate part of their built-up area as non-processing area for non-SEZ IT businesses, subject to a minimum processing area and repayment of proportionate tax benefits.

Plan for Tenants You Have Not Met Yet

A business park is judged over decades. The spine, the amenity hub, the infrastructure and the plot structure decide whether the park can keep attracting tenants as markets change. Getting those right in the master plan is what separates a lasting address from a collection of office buildings.
